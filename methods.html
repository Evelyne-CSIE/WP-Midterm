01 / FOCUS

Pomodoro Technique

Focus on one task for a short period, then take a short break. A common starting point is 25 minutes of focus and 5 minutes of rest.

Try it: Put your phone away and finish one small task before the break.

02 / MEMORY

Active Recall

Close your notes and try to remember what you studied. Checking your answer helps you notice which parts need another review.

Try it: Explain a topic without looking at your textbook.

03 / PLANNING

Time Blocking

Give different tasks a place in your schedule. Leave realistic time for classes, meals, and rest.

Try it: Reserve 30 minutes for one difficult homework question.

04 / MEMORY

Spaced Review

Return to important topics on different days instead of reviewing everything only the night before an exam.

Try it: Review today's notes again tomorrow.

05 / FOCUS

One-Task Rule

Choose one clear goal for a session. Switching between many tasks can make it harder to notice your progress.

Try it: Solve three practice problems before opening another tab.

06 / PLANNING

Break It Down

Turn a large project into smaller steps. Starting with something easy to finish can make the next step less intimidating.

Try it: Divide a report into outline, draft, and revision.

MAKE IT PERSONAL

Start with one method.

You do not need to use every study technique. Try one method for a few sessions, notice how it feels, and adjust it to suit your schedule.
